# Recipe API commercial viability (cook branch v1)

Research for issue #226 ("Which recipe API survives commercial use at scale?"), a research ticket under map #225. Verified against primary sources — official pricing pages, terms of service, and API reference docs — on 2026-07-31. These are mutable terms; reverify before a build ticket depends on them.

Cook branch v1 needs one recipe source that provides, together: structured ingredient amount + unit, serving scaling, meal-type/cuisine/diet query params, and a hero photo per recipe — and that a monetised app can legally run at production volume with shared caching.

## Spoonacular

**Licensing and pricing.** Five on-site tiers: Free ($0, 50 points/day, "Backlink Required"), Cook ($29/mo, 1,500 points/day, no backlink), Culinarian ($79/mo, 4,500 points/day), Chef ($149/mo, 10,000 points/day), Enterprise (from $300/mo, custom). ([spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing)) Nothing in the pricing FAQ or terms restricts any tier — including Free — to non-commercial use; the only tier-gated condition is the backlink requirement, which paid tiers waive. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing), FAQ "How much does the API cost?")

**Caching terms (verbatim from the Terms page).** "You may not scrape the spoonacular API or in any way attempt to copy or store the information it provides… With prior written permission from spoonacular, you may cache user-requested data to improve performance (for a maximum of 1 hour). After 1 hour, you must delete your cache and refresh the data via the spoonacular API." Exempt from that limit: "you can indefinitely store the recipe id, the recipe title, and the recipe image url on your side… you may not store any other data… including but not limited to ingredients, instructions, nutritional information." Resale of any API data is also forbidden. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/terms](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/terms), retrieved 2026-07-31)

This is the one genuine friction point: shared caching of the full recipe payload is capped at 1 hour and conditioned on **written permission** from Spoonacular — an email to send, not a wall. Nothing in the terms restricts that 1-hour cache to a single end user, so it can legitimately serve the "two people cooking the same dish should cost one lookup" requirement inside that window; id/title/image can be cached forever regardless.

**Instructions and photos in-app.** Recipes are aggregated from third-party blogs/sites; Spoonacular "always provides credit to the original source of a recipe by including the name of the website/blog along with a hyperlink," and requires the API subscriber to do the same. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/terms](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/terms)) The `Get Recipe Information` endpoint "includes instructions when available" — coverage is not 100%; a search-time `includeInstruction=true` filter exists specifically because some recipes lack them. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing) FAQ) Nothing in the terms singles out instructions or images as prohibited from in-app display, only from indefinite storage/resale — so a cooking-mode screen that shows instructions live (or within the 1-hour cache window) with source attribution is licensed.

**Data quality.** `extendedIngredients[]` returns genuinely structured `amount` and `unit` fields plus metric/US `measures` objects — real structured data, not a parsed string. ([github.com/johnwmillr/SpoonacularAPI example](https://github.com/johnwmillr/SpoonacularAPI/blob/master/spoonacular/api.py)) The database covers ~365k recipes with diet filters (ketogenic, vegan, gluten-free, paleo, primal, Whole30, etc., each defined on the pricing page's FAQ) and cuisine/meal-type parameters documented in the API reference. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing) FAQ "How are the different diets defined?"; [spoonacular.com/food-api/docs](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/docs))

**Rate limits.** Direct-from-docs: Free 60 req/min, Starter 120 req/min, Cook 5 req/sec, Culinarian 10 req/sec, Chef 20 req/sec, 429 on excess. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/docs](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/docs), "Rate Limiting & Quotas") A deck load is one `complexSearch` call, so a burst of concurrent deck loads is bounded by req/sec, not points — Cook's 5 req/sec (300/min) comfortably absorbs bursts well before the daily point quota does.

**Per-unit cost model.** A deck load = one `complexSearch` call for 15 recipes. Base cost is 1 point + 0.01 point/result. Getting structured ingredients and instructions in the same call adds `addRecipeInformation` (+0.025 pt/recipe) and `addRecipeInstructions` (+0.025 pt/recipe). ([spoonacular.com/food-api/docs](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/docs), Search Recipes quota note) For 15 recipes: 1 + (15×0.01) + (15×0.025) + (15×0.025) = **1.9 points/deck**.

On the Cook tier ($29/mo, 1,500 pts/day = 45,000 pts/30-day month), capacity is ≈23,700 decks/month before any overage — comfortably above all three volumes in the ticket:

| Cook decisions/month | Points needed | Fits in Cook's $29 quota? | Cost per decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 190 | Yes (well under 45,000) | **$0.29** (floor dominated by the $29 minimum) |
| 1,000 | 1,900 | Yes | **$0.029** |
| 10,000 | 19,000 | Yes (≈633 pts/day avg, under 1,500/day cap) | **$0.0029** |

The same $29 subscription covers all three volumes without a tier change; per-decision cost falls almost 100x from 100/mo to 10k/mo purely from spreading the fixed monthly floor — the "sticker price is misleading at low volume, near-free at scale" pattern the parent map asked to watch for. Marginal overage beyond quota is $0.005/point (Cook tier), i.e. ≈$0.0095/extra deck. ([spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing))

## Edamam

**Licensing and pricing.** developer.edamam.com's own Recipe Search API pricing table has no free production tier: Enterprise Basic $9/mo (10,000 calls/month, 10/min, "Web recipes from third parties" only, 10-day trial), Enterprise Core $99/mo (500,000 calls/month, 100/min, still third-party web recipes), Enterprise Plus $399/mo (1,000,000 calls/month, 300/min, **"Edamam-owned content with cooking instructions"**), Enterprise Unlimited (custom). All tiers require attribution (Edamam logo + link to their developer portal) and restrict use to "human, end user driven requests" — automated/programmatic collection is explicitly prohibited on every plan. ([developer.edamam.com/edamam-recipe-api](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-recipe-api))

**Instructions — the decisive gap.** Below Enterprise Plus, Edamam's own docs state plainly: "we do not provide the cooking instructions for [recipes], but we do provide the url to the source recipe." ([developer.edamam.com/edamam-docs-recipe-api](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-docs-recipe-api)) Only the $399/mo tier licenses Edamam-owned recipes with actual instructions — a 13x price jump over the entry tier just to make a future cooking-mode screen possible at all.

**Caching.** Per Edamam's API Terms of Service (edamam.com/terms/api/): data caching is not permitted "unless explicitly permitted by Edamam," and where permitted, subscribers may cache only four macro-nutrient values plus recipe URI, title, and image — and "saved data can be used only in the end user's account, behind a password." Automated/scraping requests are prohibited on every plan. This is a **hard cross-user caching ban** — cached data is scoped to the single account that fetched it, not shareable to a second user cooking the same recipe. Per the ticket's own criterion, this disqualifies Edamam regardless of price. (Terms text as indexed from [edamam.com/terms/api/](https://www.edamam.com/terms/api/); the page is JS-rendered so this is quoted via corroborating search-index extraction rather than a raw fetch — flagged for reverification before a build ticket depends on the exact wording.)

**Data quality.** Ingredients are genuinely structured (`quantity`, `measure`, `foodId`, `food`, `weight`, `foodCategory`), images ship in four sizes, and meal-type/cuisine/diet/health-label params are extensive (30+ health labels). ([developer.edamam.com/edamam-docs-recipe-api](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-docs-recipe-api)) Edamam is the best-structured data of the group — it just cannot be cached across users or (below $399/mo) shown as instructions.

## Tasty (RapidAPI, provider "apidojo")

**This is not a commercially licensed BuzzFeed/Tasty product.** The listing is provided by "apidojo," a RapidAPI publisher whose other listings are scrapers for Twitter, YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok. ([linktr.ee/apidojo](https://linktr.ee/apidojo), [apidojo.net](https://apidojo.net/)) ProgrammableWeb's own catalog entry names it the "**Unofficial** Tasty REST API v1." ([programmableweb.com/api/unofficial-tasty-rest-api-v1](https://www.programmableweb.com/api/unofficial-tasty-rest-api-v1)) There is no BuzzFeed-issued terms of service, no display-rights grant for instructions or hero photos, and no commercial license to point to — subscribing on RapidAPI (Basic $0/500 req/mo, Pro $10/mo/10,000 req, Ultra $30/mo/50,000 req, Mega $300/mo/500,000 req — [rapidapi.com/apidojo/api/tasty/pricing](https://rapidapi.com/apidojo/api/tasty/pricing)) only licenses apidojo's wrapper, not BuzzFeed's underlying recipe content or photos. Building a monetised cooking feature on unlicensed scraped content is a real legal-risk vector regardless of how good the ingredient/photo data looks — out on licensing grounds before data quality is even relevant.

## Whisk / Samsung Food

Whisk's own current developer documentation states, verbatim, on its API introduction page: **"We do not accept new API clients now."** ([docs.whisk.com/api-overview/introduction](https://docs.whisk.com/api-overview/introduction), retrieved 2026-07-31 directly from the page HTML) Whisk (acquired by Samsung, now the backend for Samsung Food) has historically operated as a B2B integration for grocers and CPG brands rather than a self-serve developer API, and that page confirms it is currently closed to new integrations of any kind. There is no price to evaluate — the platform is not accepting new commercial relationships at all. Out, full stop, unless that banner changes.

## FatSecret Platform (the fifth candidate with a genuine commercial tier)

Included because it's the other food/recipe API with a real paid commercial tier: Basic (free, self-signup, 5,000 calls/day, US-only, requires attribution), Premier Free (free for startups under $1M revenue/funding, nonprofits, students; still requires attribution), Premier (custom pricing, 58+ countries, no attribution required for paying customers). ([platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions](https://platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions)) Caching is comparatively generous — content not marked "storable indefinitely" must be refreshed within 24 hours, versus Spoonacular's 1 hour and Edamam's per-user-only restriction ([platform.fatsecret.com/terms](https://platform.fatsecret.com/terms); corroborated at [FatSecret developer forum](https://groups.google.com/g/fatsecret-platform-api/c/b4leh4EXqwo)) — and nothing found forbids showing that cache to a second user, unlike Edamam.

It still fails on data quality: `recipes.search`'s `recipe_ingredients.ingredient` is a **plain string array** ("Lemon", "2 tbsp olive oil") with no separate amount/unit fields, and `recipe_description` is a short blurb, not full directions. ([platform.fatsecret.com/docs/v2/recipes.search](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/v2/recipes.search)) There are also no documented cuisine or meal-type filters — only calorie/macro/prep-time ranges and a `must_have_images` flag (which itself implies photo coverage is partial, not universal). Out on the shopping-list-blocking requirement (structured amount + unit), independent of its otherwise-good caching terms.

## Other candidates considered and ruled out without deep review

- **Yummly** — permanently shut down December 20, 2024 after Whirlpool laid off the team; `developer.yummly.com` returns access-denied. No API to evaluate. ([Milled discontinuation notice](https://milled.com/yummly.com/important-notice-yummly-discontinuation-sF5GpZgHP5VctzYY), [updownradar.com/status/developer.yummly.com](https://updownradar.com/status/developer.yummly.com))
- **Nutritionix / Zestful** — nutrition and ingredient-parsing utilities, not recipe discovery sources with meal-type/cuisine/diet browse and hero photos; out of scope for "the one source cook-branch v1 is built on" (could resurface later as an ingredient-parser pairing if a chosen source's ingredient text needs cleanup, but Spoonacular doesn't need one).

## Recommendation

**Build cook-branch v1 on Spoonacular's Cook tier ($29/mo).** It's the only candidate that clears all five bars at once: commercially licensed with no tier restricted to non-commercial use, genuinely structured `amount`/`unit` ingredients, in-app instructions licensed (with source backlink) and available for the large majority of recipes, complete diet/cuisine/meal-type query parameters, and a caching path — a 1-hour, cross-user-legal cache once Spoonacular grants written permission — that satisfies the "one lookup per suburb" requirement well enough for how fast a cook decision actually happens. Per-decision cost falls from $0.29 at 100/month to $0.0029 at 10,000/month, entirely inside the same $29 subscription.

Edamam is the best raw *data*, but its own terms hand-cuff the two things this ticket said would decide it: caching is contractually scoped to one password-protected end user (a flat ban on the sharing this app's architecture requires), and full instructions don't exist below the $399/mo tier. Tasty (RapidAPI) isn't actually commercially licensed by BuzzFeed at any price. Whisk is currently closed to new integrations. FatSecret's ingredients are unstructured prose, which alone blocks the shopping list regardless of its comparatively good caching terms.

**The single fact that would change this pick:** if Spoonacular's response to the "prior written permission" caching request comes back restrictive — e.g. a per-app fee, a hard no on any cross-user reuse, or a cache window shortened in practice below 1 hour — the caching advantage over Edamam evaporates, and the decision should be re-opened rather than assumed. That email should be sent before this ticket is closed out into a build ticket.
